# Default Writing and Evidence Rules

Use these rules when the project does not define its own. Where the project's rules conflict with these, the project's rules win.

The reader is fluent. The goal is low cognitive load: the reader must not hold more in mind, look elsewhere more often, or reconstruct more than the content needs.

## Prose

- Put the main point first. Start each section and paragraph with its conclusion, and put the support after it.
- Introduce one new idea per sentence. A decision and its reason count as one idea. Keep sentences to about 25 words as a check, not as the goal: a short sentence with three new terms still costs too much.
- Put known information before new information. Start a sentence with something the reader already has, and end it with what is new. This order connects short sentences without extra words.
- Give each decision its reason in the same sentence: "We use X because Y."
- Use the active voice and the simple tenses.
- Use one term for one thing. Do not vary a word for style.
- Do not use idioms, metaphors, or humour.
- Delete every sentence that carries no information. Do not restate the request, do not preface a section, and do not end with a summary of what you just said.

## Evidence

- State inline the one fact that a conclusion depends on, with its date, and link to the method and data behind it. The reader then follows the argument without leaving the page.
- Mark an inference as an inference, and say what it was inferred from.
- Link to a source instead of copying it. A copied fact without its derivation goes stale with nothing to show that it has.
- Before you state a fact, search the existing docs for it. If it is already stated, link to it or reconcile the two statements.
- When two sources disagree, resolve the conflict against the primary source and remove the wrong statement. If you cannot resolve it, record the conflict, the open question, and who owns the answer.
